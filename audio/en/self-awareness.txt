The short answer
Self-awareness involves directing attention toward your own thoughts, emotions, motives, values and behaviour, including how your actions affect other people. The APA Dictionary describes self-awareness as self-focused attention or knowledge, while research distinguishes several related concepts such as self-knowledge, self-reflection and self-focused attention. APA Dictionary: Self-awareness
Self-awareness is not the same as self-knowledge
Being aware of a feeling is different from accurately understanding why you feel it. In a 2026 consensus statement, experts defined self-knowledge around the accuracy of a person's perceptions of their relatively stable characteristics and momentary states. They also emphasized that self-knowledge can be domain-specific and context-dependent, and that measuring it is difficult. Nature Reviews Psychology: 2026 consensus statement on self-knowledge
Two useful sides of self-awareness
Internal awareness: noticing thoughts, emotions, values, motives, goals and physical or emotional states.
External or interpersonal awareness: considering your behaviour and its effects on other people, including information from appropriate feedback.
A major review describes both internal and external components and notes that self-awareness can develop through reflection, experience, feedback and coaching. Annual Review: Developing Self-Awareness
Awareness does not guarantee accuracy
You can think carefully about yourself and still be mistaken. Memory, expectations, emotions, social feedback and the situation can influence self-perception. The 2026 consensus statement also notes that researchers need external criteria to evaluate accuracy and that those criteria are themselves often imperfect.
Reflection versus rumination
Useful self-reflection asks questions that can improve understanding or guide action: “What happened?”, “What was I feeling?”, “What assumption did I make?”, and “What can I do differently next time?” Endless repetitive thinking can instead keep attention locked onto the same problem without producing new information or action. Research on self-awareness specifically recognizes a potential downside of excessive introspection. Annual Review: Developing Self-Awareness
Why feedback matters
Other people do not automatically know you better than you know yourself, but appropriate feedback can reveal effects of your behaviour that are difficult to observe from your own perspective. Compare feedback with other evidence rather than accepting or rejecting it automatically.
A practical self-awareness framework
Notice: What am I feeling, thinking or doing right now?
Describe: What happened without immediately assigning a motive or label?
Interpret: What explanation am I giving the situation?
Check: What evidence supports that interpretation, and what might I be missing?
Feedback: Is there useful information from a trustworthy person or observable outcome?
Act: What small behaviour would better match my values or goal?
Review: What did I learn, and what should I change next time?
Example: “I am an angry person”
A fixed label can hide useful information. A more precise approach is to ask: “In which situations does anger appear? What happens immediately before it? What thoughts accompany it? How do I behave? What effect does that behaviour have on others? What helps me respond differently?” This turns a broad identity judgment into observable patterns that can be examined and changed.
Self-awareness in relationships
Awareness of your own reactions can make communication more deliberate. For example, noticing “I felt ignored when the message was not answered” is different from concluding “They ignored me because they do not care.” The first describes an experience; the second adds an interpretation that may or may not be correct.
Self-awareness can be developed, but not through one magic exercise
Research on self-awareness development includes reflection, feedback, coaching and educational practices, but the literature is diverse and measurement is difficult. The 2026 consensus on self-knowledge also describes change as possible in principle while noting that change can be difficult in practice. Nature Reviews Psychology, 2026
The key idea
Self-awareness is not simply “knowing yourself.” It is an ongoing process of noticing your internal experience, examining your behaviour and its effects, testing your interpretations against evidence and learning from experience. The goal is not perfect certainty about yourself; it is more accurate, flexible and useful understanding.
