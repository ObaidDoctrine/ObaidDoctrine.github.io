The short answer
Misunderstandings can arise from assumptions, different interpretations, emotional states, communication patterns and context. A single psychological explanation rarely fits every couple.
Why the simple explanation is not enough
Human behaviour is shaped by multiple interacting factors. Psychological research often examines patterns across groups and situations rather than providing a single rule that explains every individual. That is why responsible psychology communication should describe the evidence, its limits and the context in which it applies.
What to look at in real life
Consider the situation and the person’s broader context rather than one isolated behaviour.
Separate an observation from an interpretation of why it happened.
Look for patterns over time instead of treating one event as proof of a trait.
Be cautious with viral labels and absolute claims such as “always” or “never.”
Where distress or impairment is persistent, consider seeking help from an appropriately qualified professional.
What research does not automatically prove
A correlation does not by itself establish causation, and findings from one population or study do not necessarily apply identically to everyone. Evidence should be interpreted with attention to study design, population, effect size, limitations and the wider body of research.
Why context matters
Culture, relationships, environment, development and personal circumstances can change how a psychological pattern appears. Evidence-based psychological practice also emphasizes integrating research with individual characteristics, context and professional expertise when providing care.
