Relationships & Marriage

♡01Healthy communicationRelationships & Marriage

What to understand: Evidence does not support one universal communication script. Useful communication depends on the problem, context and relationship pattern.

Practical step: Describe one specific event, its impact, the change you want, and invite the other person’s perspective.

When to seek help: If discussion includes threats, coercion, intimidation or violence, prioritize safety and specialist support.

◉02Emotional neglectRelationships & Marriage

What to understand: A persistent pattern of important emotional needs being ignored or dismissed is different from an occasional missed need; duration and context matter.

Practical step: Track concrete events, what you asked for, the response, and whether behaviour changes over time.

When to seek help: If neglect co-occurs with fear, isolation, coercive control or abuse, seek confidential support.

＋03Relationship boundariesRelationships & Marriage

What to understand: A boundary states what you will accept and what you will do when a limit is crossed; it is not a demand that another person obey.

Practical step: State one clear limit and a consequence you control, then apply it consistently.

When to seek help: If boundary-setting could trigger retaliation or violence, make a safety plan first.

⌁04Controlling behaviourRelationships & Marriage

What to understand: Control can include monitoring, isolation, restrictions on money or movement, threats and pressure over relationships. Patterns matter more than one disagreement.

Practical step: Record observable behaviours rather than relying only on labels; keep key contacts and documents accessible if safe.

When to seek help: Threats, intimidation, stalking, isolation or escalating control can indicate a safety risk.

◆05Conflict repairRelationships & Marriage

What to understand: Repair is about restoring safety and cooperation after conflict, not pretending the disagreement never happened.

Practical step: Return after emotions settle, acknowledge your part, clarify needs and agree on one specific change.

When to seek help: If conflict repeatedly becomes threatening or physically unsafe, ordinary communication advice is insufficient.

⌂06Trust after hurtRelationships & Marriage

What to understand: Trust generally rebuilds through repeated experiences of accountability, transparency and consistent behaviour rather than promises alone.

Practical step: Define observable actions that would rebuild trust and review whether behaviour matches commitments.

When to seek help: If the harm involves violence, coercion or ongoing safety risks, seek qualified support before reconciliation.

✿07Marriage expectationsRelationships & Marriage

What to understand: Expectations about money, household work, family involvement, privacy, parenting, intimacy and careers can differ between partners and cultures.

Practical step: List major expectations, identify what is negotiable, and make explicit agreements instead of relying on assumptions.

When to seek help: For severe conflict or safety concerns, professional support may help when joint work is safe.

🛡08In-law boundariesRelationships & Marriage

What to understand: Extended-family relationships can coexist with clear couple boundaries around privacy, decisions and responsibilities.

Practical step: Agree on shared boundaries with your partner first, then communicate them respectfully and consistently.

When to seek help: If family pressure becomes threatening, isolating, financially controlling or violent, prioritize safety.

◇09Separation & adjustmentRelationships & Marriage

What to understand: Separation can involve grief, uncertainty, practical disruption and changes in identity or routine; adjustment has no single timetable.

Practical step: Stabilize sleep, food, housing, finances, social contact and daily routine before tackling every longer-term decision.

When to seek help: Seek professional, legal or safety support when distress is severe or children, violence or housing risks are involved.

✦10Healthy vs unhealthy patternsRelationships & Marriage

What to understand: Healthy relationships can contain conflict. Useful markers include respect, autonomy, accountability, repair and freedom to disagree without fear or coercion.

Practical step: Review repeated patterns over time rather than judging a relationship from one incident.

When to seek help: Fear, threats, stalking, coercive control or violence are safety concerns, not simply communication problems.

Mental & Emotional Wellbeing

♡11OverthinkingMental & Emotional Wellbeing

What to understand: Overthinking can become unhelpful when repeated worry or rumination is difficult to control and starts interfering with daily life; it is not automatically a disorder.

Practical step: Write down the worry, separate what you can control from what you cannot, and choose one concrete next action or a scheduled time to revisit it.

When to seek help: Seek professional help if persistent worry or rumination is disrupting sleep, work, relationships or daily functioning.

◉12Anxiety basicsMental & Emotional Wellbeing

What to understand: Anxiety is a normal response to stress, but an anxiety disorder involves excessive or persistent fear or worry that causes significant distress or interferes with life.

Practical step: Notice triggers, body sensations and avoidance patterns; use evidence-based coping such as regular sleep, activity, calming exercises and supportive connection.

When to seek help: Talk with a qualified health professional when anxiety persists, escalates or substantially affects daily life.

＋13Low moodMental & Emotional Wellbeing

What to understand: Feeling sad or low is different from a depressive episode. Depression involves persistent symptoms such as low mood or loss of interest plus other symptoms that affect functioning.

Practical step: Keep basic routines where possible, stay connected, continue manageable activities and record how symptoms change over time.

When to seek help: Seek professional care when symptoms persist, impair functioning, or include hopelessness or thoughts of self-harm.

⌁14LonelinessMental & Emotional Wellbeing

What to understand: Loneliness is a painful sense of insufficient connection; being alone and feeling lonely are not always the same. Social context, life transitions and mental health can interact.

Practical step: Choose one low-pressure connection: message someone trusted, join a group, or arrange a regular shared activity rather than waiting for motivation to appear.

When to seek help: Seek support if loneliness is persistent, worsening, or accompanied by significant depression, anxiety or thoughts of self-harm.

◆15BurnoutMental & Emotional Wellbeing

What to understand: Burnout is associated with chronic workplace stress that has not been successfully managed and is characterized by exhaustion, increased mental distance or cynicism, and reduced professional efficacy.

Practical step: Identify the main work stressors, set realistic boundaries, protect recovery time and discuss workload or role changes where possible.

When to seek help: Seek professional support if exhaustion or distress is severe, persistent, or affecting health and functioning; do not assume every form of distress is burnout.

⌂16Self-worthMental & Emotional Wellbeing

What to understand: Self-worth can fluctuate with relationships, achievement, social comparison and life events. Low self-worth is not proof that negative self-judgments are accurate.

Practical step: Track self-critical thoughts and test them against evidence; build identity around values, skills, relationships and activities rather than a single outcome.

When to seek help: Seek help if low self-worth is persistent and linked with depression, self-harm, eating concerns or major impairment.

✿17Emotional regulationMental & Emotional Wellbeing

What to understand: Emotional regulation means influencing how emotions are noticed, understood and expressed; it does not mean suppressing or eliminating emotions.

Practical step: Pause before reacting, name the emotion, identify the trigger and choose a response consistent with your values and the situation.

When to seek help: Seek professional support if emotions feel uncontrollable, cause repeated harm, or seriously disrupt relationships, work or safety.

🛡18Stress managementMental & Emotional Wellbeing

What to understand: Stress is a physical or mental response to an external cause. It can be short-term or repeated, while anxiety can persist even without a current threat.

Practical step: Identify the stressor, reduce avoidable demands, protect sleep and regular meals, move your body, and use supportive or relaxation strategies that work for you.

When to seek help: Seek professional help if symptoms do not settle, interfere with everyday life, or lead to persistent avoidance or severe distress.

◇19Sleep & wellbeingMental & Emotional Wellbeing

What to understand: Sleep and mental wellbeing influence one another. Persistent sleep problems can accompany stress, anxiety or depression and may also have physical causes.

Practical step: Keep a consistent sleep/wake routine, reduce late stimulating activity, and track sleep patterns; avoid treating persistent insomnia with random online remedies.

When to seek help: Talk to a health professional for persistent sleep problems, major daytime impairment, or symptoms suggesting another medical or mental health condition.

✦20When to seek professional helpMental & Emotional Wellbeing

What to understand: Professional support is appropriate when distress is persistent, severe, difficult to manage, or interferes with daily functioning. Crisis symptoms require urgent help.

Practical step: Prepare a short record of symptoms, duration, triggers, medicines and questions, then contact a qualified clinician or mental-health service.

When to seek help: Seek urgent local emergency or crisis support for immediate danger, suicide risk, or inability to stay safe.

Women’s Health

♡21Menstrual healthWomen’s Health

What to understand: Menstrual health includes accurate information, dignity, access to appropriate materials and care, and the ability to participate in daily life. Cycles vary, but persistent or disruptive symptoms deserve attention.

Practical step: Track cycle timing, bleeding, pain and other symptoms; use reliable menstrual-health information and seek care when symptoms are unusual or disruptive.

When to seek help: Seek medical assessment for very heavy, unpredictable, absent or very painful bleeding, or symptoms that interfere with school, work or daily life.

◉22Period painWomen’s Health

What to understand: Some menstrual pain is common, but severe or disabling pain is not something to simply normalize. Persistent pain can occur with conditions such as endometriosis or other disorders.

Practical step: Record pain severity, timing, bleeding and associated symptoms and discuss the pattern with a clinician; use only treatments appropriate for you.

When to seek help: Seek medical care when pain is severe, worsening, occurs outside menstruation, or prevents normal activities.

＋23PMSWomen’s Health

What to understand: Premenstrual symptoms can include physical and emotional changes before menstruation. Severity and pattern matter, and symptoms that substantially impair life should be assessed.

Practical step: Track symptoms across several cycles and note their timing, severity and effect on daily activities rather than relying on a single month.

When to seek help: Seek professional assessment when symptoms are severe, recurrent and disruptive, or when mood symptoms are difficult to manage.

⌁24PCOS basicsWomen’s Health

What to understand: PCOS is a common hormonal and metabolic condition. WHO estimates about 10–13% of reproductive-aged women are affected and up to 70% may be undiagnosed; symptoms vary and diagnosis requires clinical assessment.

Practical step: Record cycle patterns and symptoms such as acne, excess hair or fertility concerns, then discuss them with a qualified clinician rather than diagnosing from one symptom or an online checklist.

When to seek help: Seek medical assessment for persistent irregular or absent periods, fertility concerns or other symptoms suggestive of PCOS.

◆25Endometriosis awarenessWomen’s Health

What to understand: Endometriosis is a chronic condition in which tissue similar to the uterine lining grows outside the uterus. WHO estimates it affects about 10% of reproductive-aged women and notes that diagnosis can be delayed.

Practical step: Keep a detailed symptom record including pain, bleeding, bowel/bladder symptoms and impact on work or school, then share it with a clinician.

When to seek help: Seek medical evaluation for severe menstrual or pelvic pain, heavy bleeding, persistent pelvic symptoms or fertility concerns.

⌂26Pregnancy questionsWomen’s Health

What to understand: Pregnancy care involves evidence-based antenatal monitoring, nutrition, medicines and attention to warning signs; individual recommendations vary by health history and setting.

Practical step: Write down questions, medicines, symptoms and pregnancy dates and use qualified antenatal care and current local guidance rather than social-media advice.

When to seek help: Urgent assessment is needed for serious warning signs such as heavy bleeding, severe pain, severe headache/vision changes, seizures, breathing difficulty or other acute concerns.

✿27Postpartum recoveryWomen’s Health

What to understand: Postpartum recovery is a health period, not just a return to normal routine. WHO emphasizes ongoing physical, emotional and social support for women and newborns, including postnatal care.

Practical step: Arrange postnatal follow-up, prioritize rest, nutrition, support and gradual recovery, and discuss physical or emotional symptoms with a health professional.

When to seek help: Seek urgent care for heavy bleeding, severe pain, fever with concerning symptoms, breathing problems, chest pain, severe headache/vision changes, or thoughts of self-harm or harming the baby.

🛡28Breast health awarenessWomen’s Health

What to understand: Breast changes have many possible causes, and most changes are not cancer, but a new or persistent change should be assessed rather than self-diagnosed.

Practical step: Become familiar with what is normal for you and report a new lump, skin or nipple change, unusual discharge, or persistent focal pain to a clinician.

When to seek help: Seek prompt medical assessment for a new persistent lump or other concerning change, especially if it is worsening.

◇29Cervical healthWomen’s Health

What to understand: Cervical cancer is largely preventable through vaccination against HPV, screening and timely treatment of precancerous changes. Screening recommendations vary by country, age and risk.

Practical step: Follow your country’s current cervical-screening and HPV-vaccination recommendations and discuss personal risk factors with a qualified clinician.

When to seek help: Seek medical care for unusual vaginal bleeding, bleeding after sex, persistent unusual discharge or pelvic symptoms; screening is preventive and does not replace symptom evaluation.

✦30Preventive check-upsWomen’s Health

What to understand: Preventive care is not one identical checklist for every woman. Recommended screening and vaccination depend on age, pregnancy status, family history, risk factors and country guidelines.

Practical step: Keep a personal preventive-care record and use current national or professional guidelines to identify vaccinations and screenings relevant to you.

When to seek help: Ask a qualified health professional about overdue or unclear preventive care, especially with family history or new symptoms.

Digital Safety

♡31Online harassmentDigital Safety

What to understand: Online harassment can include repeated unwanted messages, threats, coordinated abuse, doxxing or other harmful conduct. The online/offline boundary can be porous, so context and escalation matter.

Practical step: Save relevant messages, URLs and account details; use platform block/report tools and review privacy settings. Avoid engaging with an escalating harasser.

When to seek help: If there are credible threats, doxxing, stalking, extortion or immediate physical-safety concerns, involve a trusted person and appropriate official or specialist support.

◉32CyberstalkingDigital Safety

What to understand: Cyberstalking involves repeated unwanted monitoring, contact or surveillance through digital tools that can cause fear or serious disruption.

Practical step: Preserve evidence, secure accounts and devices, review location sharing and login sessions, and tell someone you trust.

When to seek help: Seek urgent help when threats appear credible, the person is trying to locate you physically, or online conduct is escalating toward offline danger.

＋33Fake accountsDigital Safety

What to understand: Fake profiles can be used for impersonation, scams, harassment, fraud or unwanted contact. A profile photo or follower count does not prove identity.

Practical step: Verify identity through an independent channel before sharing sensitive information; save the profile URL and report impersonation or abuse to the platform.

When to seek help: Seek financial, legal or cyber-support help when a fake account is linked to fraud, threats, blackmail or significant reputational harm.

⌁34ImpersonationDigital Safety

What to understand: Impersonation occurs when someone presents themselves as you or uses your identity to mislead others. It can overlap with fraud, harassment or account takeover.

Practical step: Capture the profile URL, screenshots and relevant messages; report the account, alert important contacts if needed, and secure your real accounts.

When to seek help: Use appropriate cybercrime or legal support when impersonation involves fraud, threats, financial loss or persistent targeted abuse.

◆35Image-based abuseDigital Safety

What to understand: Image-based abuse includes sharing or threatening to share intimate or private images without consent. The harm can continue through copying and redistribution.

Practical step: Do not forward the material or pay a blackmailer. Preserve evidence, report the content through the platform and seek support from someone you trust.

When to seek help: Seek urgent specialist or official support if there is coercion, extortion, stalking, threats, or concern for physical safety.

⌂36Deepfake awarenessDigital Safety

What to understand: Deepfakes are digitally altered or AI-generated media that can make a person appear to say or do something they did not. They can be used for harassment, fraud or non-consensual sexual abuse.

Practical step: Check the original source, context and date; compare with trusted reporting and avoid amplifying suspicious content. If possible, preserve the original URL and file information.

When to seek help: Report abusive synthetic media and seek specialist or official support when it targets you, involves sexual content, threats, fraud or coordinated harassment.

✿37Blackmail & sextortionDigital Safety

What to understand: Sextortion is coercion using intimate images, recordings or claims about them to demand money, more content or other actions. Manipulated or AI-generated material can also be used.

Practical step: Do not pay or send more material. Stop engaging where safe, preserve evidence, block/report the account and tell a trusted person.

When to seek help: If threats are credible, a child is involved, or there is immediate danger, seek urgent local emergency, child-protection or cybercrime support.

🛡38Account securityDigital Safety

What to understand: Strong account security reduces the chance that attackers can access personal data, messages or recovery channels. Passwords alone provide weaker protection than layered authentication.

Practical step: Use a unique long password or passphrase, a password manager, MFA or a passkey where available, current software and secure recovery methods.

When to seek help: If an account may be compromised, change credentials from a trusted device, revoke unfamiliar sessions, secure recovery email/phone and use the platform's account-recovery process.

◇39Privacy settingsDigital Safety

What to understand: Privacy controls can reduce unnecessary exposure of posts, profile information, location, tags, messages and connected apps, but settings do not make a platform completely private.

Practical step: Audit audience settings, location sharing, tagging, direct-message controls, app permissions and old public posts; repeat the review after major platform changes.

When to seek help: If someone is targeting or monitoring you, tighten exposure, preserve evidence and seek specialist support rather than relying only on privacy settings.

✦40Evidence preservationDigital Safety

What to understand: Digital evidence can disappear, be edited or become harder to authenticate after an account or post changes. Useful records can include original messages, URLs, timestamps and account identifiers.

Practical step: Keep original files where possible, take clear screenshots, record dates and URLs, and store copies somewhere secure. Avoid unnecessary editing or public reposting.

When to seek help: For serious threats, extortion, stalking or legal action, ask a qualified legal or cyber-support service how to preserve evidence appropriately in your jurisdiction.
