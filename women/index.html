Digital Safety

♡1Online harassmentDigital Safety

What to understand: Online harassment can include repeated unwanted messages, threats, coordinated abuse, doxxing or other harmful conduct. The online/offline boundary can be porous, so context and escalation matter.

Practical step: Save relevant messages, URLs and account details; use platform block/report tools and review privacy settings. Avoid engaging with an escalating harasser.

When to seek help: If there are credible threats, doxxing, stalking, extortion or immediate physical-safety concerns, involve a trusted person and appropriate official or specialist support.

◉2CyberstalkingDigital Safety

What to understand: Cyberstalking involves repeated unwanted monitoring, contact or surveillance through digital tools that can cause fear or serious disruption.

Practical step: Preserve evidence, secure accounts and devices, review location sharing and login sessions, and tell someone you trust.

When to seek help: Seek urgent help when threats appear credible, the person is trying to locate you physically, or online conduct is escalating toward offline danger.

＋3Fake accountsDigital Safety

What to understand: Fake profiles can be used for impersonation, scams, harassment, fraud or unwanted contact. A profile photo or follower count does not prove identity.

Practical step: Verify identity through an independent channel before sharing sensitive information; save the profile URL and report impersonation or abuse to the platform.

When to seek help: Seek financial, legal or cyber-support help when a fake account is linked to fraud, threats, blackmail or significant reputational harm.

⌁4ImpersonationDigital Safety

What to understand: Impersonation occurs when someone presents themselves as you or uses your identity to mislead others. It can overlap with fraud, harassment or account takeover.

Practical step: Capture the profile URL, screenshots and relevant messages; report the account, alert important contacts if needed, and secure your real accounts.

When to seek help: Use appropriate cybercrime or legal support when impersonation involves fraud, threats, financial loss or persistent targeted abuse.

◆5Image-based abuseDigital Safety

What to understand: Image-based abuse includes sharing or threatening to share intimate or private images without consent. The harm can continue through copying and redistribution.

Practical step: Do not forward the material or pay a blackmailer. Preserve evidence, report the content through the platform and seek support from someone you trust.

When to seek help: Seek urgent specialist or official support if there is coercion, extortion, stalking, threats, or concern for physical safety.

⌂6Deepfake awarenessDigital Safety

What to understand: Deepfakes are digitally altered or AI-generated media that can make a person appear to say or do something they did not. They can be used for harassment, fraud or non-consensual sexual abuse.

Practical step: Check the original source, context and date; compare with trusted reporting and avoid amplifying suspicious content. If possible, preserve the original URL and file information.

When to seek help: Report abusive synthetic media and seek specialist or official support when it targets you, involves sexual content, threats, fraud or coordinated harassment.

✿7Blackmail & sextortionDigital Safety

What to understand: Sextortion is coercion using intimate images, recordings or claims about them to demand money, more content or other actions. Manipulated or AI-generated material can also be used.

Practical step: Do not pay or send more material. Stop engaging where safe, preserve evidence, block/report the account and tell a trusted person.

When to seek help: If threats are credible, a child is involved, or there is immediate danger, seek urgent local emergency, child-protection or cybercrime support.

🛡8Account securityDigital Safety

What to understand: Strong account security reduces the chance that attackers can access personal data, messages or recovery channels. Passwords alone provide weaker protection than layered authentication.

Practical step: Use a unique long password or passphrase, a password manager, MFA or a passkey where available, current software and secure recovery methods.

When to seek help: If an account may be compromised, change credentials from a trusted device, revoke unfamiliar sessions, secure recovery email/phone and use the platform's account-recovery process.

◇9Privacy settingsDigital Safety

What to understand: Privacy controls can reduce unnecessary exposure of posts, profile information, location, tags, messages and connected apps, but settings do not make a platform completely private.

Practical step: Audit audience settings, location sharing, tagging, direct-message controls, app permissions and old public posts; repeat the review after major platform changes.

When to seek help: If someone is targeting or monitoring you, tighten exposure, preserve evidence and seek specialist support rather than relying only on privacy settings.

✦10Evidence preservationDigital Safety

What to understand: Digital evidence can disappear, be edited or become harder to authenticate after an account or post changes. Useful records can include original messages, URLs, timestamps and account identifiers.

Practical step: Keep original files where possible, take clear screenshots, record dates and URLs, and store copies somewhere secure. Avoid unnecessary editing or public reposting.

When to seek help: For serious threats, extortion, stalking or legal action, ask a qualified legal or cyber-support service how to preserve evidence appropriately in your jurisdiction.

Career & Money

♡11Career after marriageCareer & Money

What to understand: Marriage can change time, care responsibilities, mobility, finances and career plans. Career decisions are shaped by both personal circumstances and wider workplace or legal conditions.

Practical step: Map skills, income needs, care responsibilities, location constraints and preferred work options; discuss practical expectations with relevant family members or partners.

When to seek help: Seek professional career, financial or legal advice when workplace rights, discrimination, safety or major financial decisions are involved.

◉12Returning to workCareer & Money

What to understand: Returning after caregiving, childbirth, illness or a career break can involve skill refresh, confidence rebuilding, childcare and changes in the labour market.

Practical step: Update your CV and skills, reconnect with professional networks, identify realistic roles and plan a gradual re-entry if that fits your circumstances.

When to seek help: Use employment or legal support if you face unlawful discrimination, harassment, unsafe conditions or disputes over workplace rights.

＋13Workplace boundariesCareer & Money

What to understand: Healthy boundaries clarify availability, responsibilities, communication and acceptable behaviour without preventing legitimate teamwork.

Practical step: Define working hours and communication channels, document important agreements and say clearly when a request conflicts with your role or capacity.

When to seek help: Seek HR, union, legal or other qualified support when boundary violations involve retaliation, harassment, discrimination or safety concerns.

⌁14Workplace harassmentCareer & Money

What to understand: Workplace harassment can include unwanted conduct that humiliates, intimidates, threatens or creates a hostile environment; definitions and legal remedies vary by jurisdiction.

Practical step: Record dates, people involved, messages and witnesses where safe; check your employer's policy and current local reporting options.

When to seek help: Seek prompt support when harassment is sexual, threatening, retaliatory, discriminatory or affecting your safety or ability to work.

◆15Remote workCareer & Money

What to understand: Remote work can improve flexibility for some people but can also blur work-life boundaries, increase isolation or create unequal access to visibility and opportunities.

Practical step: Set a defined workspace or work period where possible, use clear communication norms and track workload and outcomes rather than staying constantly online.

When to seek help: Seek workplace support when remote arrangements create excessive workload, discriminatory treatment, privacy risks or unsafe working conditions.

⌂16Freelancing basicsCareer & Money

What to understand: Freelancing is self-employment through project or contract work and can offer flexibility while also shifting responsibility for income, contracts, taxes and benefits to the worker.

Practical step: Start with one clear service, a portfolio, written scope and payment terms; verify clients and keep records of invoices, expenses and agreements.

When to seek help: Seek qualified tax, legal or financial advice when contracts, cross-border payments, business registration or tax obligations are unclear.

✿17Digital skillsCareer & Money

What to understand: Digital skills range from basic online safety and productivity to communication, data, design, coding and AI literacy. The right skills depend on the target role or business.

Practical step: Choose one job-relevant skill, use reputable learning resources and build a small demonstrable project rather than collecting certificates without practice.

When to seek help: Seek career guidance when you are unsure which skills match local demand, your experience or a specific career path.

🛡18Financial literacyCareer & Money

What to understand: Financial literacy includes understanding income, expenses, saving, debt, interest, risk, insurance and basic investing. Advice should account for local laws and financial products.

Practical step: Track cash flow, build an emergency buffer where feasible, compare fees and interest, and avoid financial decisions based only on viral advice.

When to seek help: Use a qualified financial or legal professional for complex investments, debt restructuring, tax matters or products you do not understand.

◇19Saving & budgetingCareer & Money

What to understand: A budget is a planning tool, not a measure of personal worth. Income volatility, caregiving and emergencies can make simple monthly targets unrealistic.

Practical step: List essential and flexible expenses, set a realistic savings goal and review the plan regularly; separate emergency savings from money needed soon.

When to seek help: Seek debt or financial counselling when repayments are becoming unmanageable, essential needs are at risk, or you are considering high-risk borrowing.

✦20Starting a small businessCareer & Money

What to understand: Starting a business involves validating a real customer need, costs, pricing, legal structure, records and risk management. Women may also face unequal access to finance and support.

Practical step: Test the idea cheaply, define the customer and offer, calculate basic unit economics, keep business and personal money records separate, and check local registration requirements.

When to seek help: Seek qualified business, tax, legal or financing support before signing major contracts, taking substantial debt or expanding beyond your capacity.

Family & Motherhood

♡21Motherhood pressureFamily & Motherhood

What to understand: Motherhood can involve physical, emotional, financial and time demands. Pressure is shaped by family expectations, resources, work and social support; there is no single 'correct' way to be a mother.

Practical step: Separate essential responsibilities from unrealistic expectations, identify what can be shared or delayed, and schedule recovery time where possible.

When to seek help: Seek professional support when distress is persistent, functioning is affected, or pressure is linked with abuse, severe anxiety/depression or safety concerns.

◉22Mother guiltFamily & Motherhood

What to understand: Guilt can arise when a mother believes she has fallen short of expectations, even when circumstances are difficult. Feeling guilty does not by itself prove that she has done something wrong.

Practical step: Name the specific expectation, check whether it is realistic, and focus on the child's actual needs and your available resources rather than perfection.

When to seek help: Seek support if guilt becomes persistent, overwhelming, or is accompanied by depression, anxiety, self-harm thoughts or major impairment.

＋23Parenting stressFamily & Motherhood

What to understand: Parenting stress can increase when demands exceed available time, money, sleep, support or coping capacity. Stress is not evidence of being a bad parent.

Practical step: Identify the biggest pressure point, simplify one routine, share age-appropriate tasks and ask trusted people for concrete help.

When to seek help: Seek professional help when stress feels uncontrollable, leads to harmful behaviour, severe distress or significant problems in family functioning.

⌁24Co-parentingFamily & Motherhood

What to understand: Co-parenting works best when adults communicate about the child's needs, responsibilities and routines while keeping conflict away from the child as much as possible.

Practical step: Agree on practical responsibilities, schedules, school/health information and communication methods; document important arrangements when necessary.

When to seek help: Use family, legal or safeguarding support when there is persistent conflict, coercive control, violence, safety risk or disputes affecting the child.

◆25Daughter safetyFamily & Motherhood

What to understand: Girls' safety includes protection from violence, exploitation, harassment, unsafe online contact and harmful environments while preserving age-appropriate autonomy.

Practical step: Teach body and online safety in age-appropriate language, establish trusted adults and emergency contacts, and take disclosures seriously without blaming the child.

When to seek help: Seek urgent safeguarding or emergency help for abuse, exploitation, credible threats, missing-child risk or immediate danger.

⌂26Family expectationsFamily & Motherhood

What to understand: Family expectations about marriage, caregiving, education, work and parenting can support people but can also create pressure when they conflict with a woman's needs or rights.

Practical step: Separate shared values from demands that are negotiable; communicate priorities, limits and practical constraints clearly.

When to seek help: Seek confidential support when expectations involve coercion, threats, forced decisions, violence or serious interference with education, work or safety.

✿27Caregiving loadFamily & Motherhood

What to understand: Caregiving includes physical, practical and emotional work for children, older people, people with disabilities or relatives with health needs. Unequal care can reduce time for paid work, education and rest.

Practical step: List recurring care tasks, estimate the time they require, and redistribute tasks or seek formal/community support where available.

When to seek help: Seek health, social or financial support when caregiving causes exhaustion, injury, unsafe care, severe distress or loss of essential income.

🛡28Healthy family boundariesFamily & Motherhood

What to understand: Family boundaries define privacy, decision-making, responsibilities and acceptable communication. Boundaries can exist alongside closeness and respect.

Practical step: Choose one boundary, state it clearly, decide what you will do if it is crossed, and apply it consistently where safe.

When to seek help: Seek support if boundary-setting leads to threats, violence, stalking, financial control or other retaliation.

◇29Teen girls & parentsFamily & Motherhood

What to understand: Adolescence involves growing independence, changing relationships and developing decision-making skills. Safety and connection are more useful than constant surveillance alone.

Practical step: Use age-appropriate conversations about online safety, relationships, health and school; agree on clear rules and allow appropriate privacy.

When to seek help: Seek professional or safeguarding help when there are signs of abuse, exploitation, self-harm risk, severe mental distress or unsafe online contact.

✦30New-mother supportFamily & Motherhood

What to understand: The postnatal period involves physical recovery, sleep disruption, feeding demands, emotional changes and adjustment. WHO emphasizes integrated physical, mental and social support after birth.

Practical step: Arrange practical help with food, rest, household tasks and baby care; attend postnatal checks and discuss mood or anxiety symptoms with a health professional.

When to seek help: Urgent care is needed for heavy bleeding, severe physical symptoms, acute mental-health crisis, thoughts of self-harm or harm to the baby, or other emergency warning signs.

Body Image & Social Media

♡31Body imageBody Image & Social Media

What to understand: Body image is how a person perceives and feels about their body. It can be influenced by peers, culture, media and life stage; dissatisfaction can occur across body sizes and backgrounds.

Practical step: Notice appearance-focused thoughts and unfollow or mute accounts that repeatedly trigger comparison. Build activities and goals not centered on appearance.

When to seek help: Seek professional support if body concerns cause major distress, avoidance, compulsive checking or eating-related problems.

◉32Beauty pressureBody Image & Social Media

What to understand: Beauty standards vary across cultures and time, while commercial and social-media content can amplify narrow ideals.

Practical step: Ask who benefits from a beauty claim, check whether images are edited or sponsored, and choose personal grooming goals that fit your values and resources.

When to seek help: Seek support when appearance pressure is causing severe distress, harmful dieting, unsafe procedures or major impairment.

＋33Social comparisonBody Image & Social Media

What to understand: Comparing yourself with others is common, but online feeds often show selected and edited moments rather than a complete life.

Practical step: Use a short feed audit: note which accounts leave you informed or supported and mute or unfollow repeatedly harmful comparison triggers.

When to seek help: Seek help if comparison is linked with persistent low mood, anxiety, disordered eating or significant withdrawal from life.

⌁34Filters & edited imagesBody Image & Social Media

What to understand: Filters, retouching, lighting, posing and generative editing can change appearance substantially. A polished image is not reliable evidence of someone's everyday appearance.

Practical step: Treat highly edited content as media, not a benchmark; check context and avoid comparing your unedited body with edited images.

When to seek help: Seek professional support if edited-image exposure is driving severe body distress, compulsive checking or harmful eating/exercise behaviour.

◆35Influencer cultureBody Image & Social Media

What to understand: Influencers can provide education, community and entertainment, but commercial incentives, sponsorships and selective self-presentation can shape what audiences see.

Practical step: Check whether a post is sponsored or selling something, look for transparent sources, and separate a creator's personal story from general health evidence.

When to seek help: For health, nutrition, weight-loss or cosmetic claims, use qualified professionals and authoritative sources rather than influencer advice alone.

⌂36Self-acceptanceBody Image & Social Media

What to understand: Self-acceptance does not require liking every aspect of your appearance; it can mean treating your body with respect while allowing normal changes.

Practical step: Shift one goal from appearance to function, comfort, health or valued activity; use neutral language when talking about your body.

When to seek help: Seek help when self-criticism becomes persistent, disabling or connected to depression, anxiety or eating concerns.

✿37Appearance anxietyBody Image & Social Media

What to understand: Appearance anxiety can involve persistent worry about perceived flaws, checking, reassurance-seeking or avoiding people and situations. It can overlap with mental-health conditions and is not diagnosed from one symptom.

Practical step: Track when the worry appears and reduce repetitive checking or reassurance loops; discuss persistent symptoms with a qualified mental-health professional.

When to seek help: Seek professional assessment when appearance worries consume substantial time, cause avoidance or interfere with school, work, relationships or daily life.

🛡38Healthy media habitsBody Image & Social Media

What to understand: Digital media can provide connection and useful information, but effects vary by person, content and context; evidence does not support one universal screen-time rule for everyone.

Practical step: Curate your feed, disable unnecessary notifications, create device-free periods and protect sleep, meals, study/work and offline relationships.

When to seek help: Seek help when digital use feels difficult to control or consistently worsens sleep, mood, eating, relationships or daily functioning.

◇39Confidence beyond appearanceBody Image & Social Media

What to understand: Confidence can grow from skills, values, relationships, contribution and problem-solving—not appearance alone.

Practical step: Set a weekly goal based on something you want to learn, contribute or experience; record progress without rating your looks.

When to seek help: Seek support if appearance remains the main source of self-worth and this causes persistent distress or functional problems.

✦40Eating concerns: when to seek helpBody Image & Social Media

What to understand: Eating concerns can range from irregular patterns to serious eating disorders. Adolescence is an important period for early recognition, and girls are commonly affected by eating disorders.

Practical step: Avoid self-diagnosis or crash diets; note changes in eating, weight-related thoughts, exercise, physical symptoms and distress, and discuss concerns with a qualified professional.

When to seek help: Seek prompt professional care for significant restriction, bingeing, purging, fainting, rapid physical changes, severe distress or self-harm/suicide risk.

Rights, Safety & Legal Awareness

♡41Know your rightsRights, Safety & Legal Awareness

What to understand: Rights differ by country and legal system. International standards and global datasets can help explain patterns, but they do not replace current local law or legal advice.

Practical step: Identify the country and issue, then check a current government, court, ombuds, legal-aid or other authoritative source before acting.

When to seek help: Seek qualified legal or rights support when a decision involves safety, custody, employment, housing, immigration, property or another high-stakes matter.

◉42Domestic violence awarenessRights, Safety & Legal Awareness

What to understand: Domestic violence can include physical, sexual, psychological or economic abuse and controlling behaviour. Definitions and remedies vary by jurisdiction.

Practical step: If safe, document incidents and identify a trusted person, local specialist service and emergency option; prioritize safety over confronting an abusive person.

When to seek help: Seek urgent help for immediate danger, serious injury, threats, stalking, coercive control or risk to children or other dependents.

＋43Sexual harassment awarenessRights, Safety & Legal Awareness

What to understand: Sexual harassment can occur in workplaces, education, public spaces and online environments. Legal definitions and reporting routes vary by country.

Practical step: Keep relevant messages or records where safe, review the institution's policy and identify an appropriate reporting or support channel.

When to seek help: Seek prompt support when harassment includes threats, retaliation, unwanted sexual contact, stalking or a safety concern.

⌁44Child marriage awarenessRights, Safety & Legal Awareness

What to understand: Child marriage is associated with risks to education, health, autonomy and protection from violence. The legal minimum age and exceptions vary across countries.

Practical step: Check the current law and child-protection framework in the relevant jurisdiction; involve trusted safeguarding professionals rather than relying on social-media advice.

When to seek help: Seek urgent child-protection or legal help if a child faces forced marriage, threats, abduction or immediate danger.

◆45Workplace rightsRights, Safety & Legal Awareness

What to understand: Workplace rights can cover equal treatment, pay, leave, safety, freedom from violence and harassment, and access to complaint mechanisms, but rules differ by jurisdiction and contract.

Practical step: Keep a copy of contracts and policies, record relevant events, and verify rights through current labour authorities, unions, ombuds services or qualified advisers.

When to seek help: Seek qualified employment/legal support for dismissal disputes, discrimination, wage issues, harassment, retaliation or unsafe conditions.

⌂46Digital rightsRights, Safety & Legal Awareness

What to understand: Digital rights include privacy, data protection, access to information, expression and protection from unlawful abuse; exact rights depend on jurisdiction and platform rules.

Practical step: Review privacy settings and platform controls, understand what data you consent to share, and use official complaint or data-protection channels where available.

When to seek help: Seek specialist cyber, legal or privacy support for serious doxxing, identity theft, threats, unlawful data exposure or persistent abuse.

✿47Privacy and consentRights, Safety & Legal Awareness

What to understand: Consent should be informed, voluntary and specific; privacy expectations also vary by context and law. Sharing or recording personal or intimate material can create serious harm and legal consequences.

Practical step: Before sharing another person's information, image or recording, consider whether they agreed and whether the context changed; use privacy-preserving defaults.

When to seek help: Seek professional help when consent is disputed, intimate material is shared without permission, or coercion, threats or exploitation are involved.

🛡48Safety planningRights, Safety & Legal Awareness

What to understand: A safety plan is a practical, individualized set of steps for reducing risk and reaching support. It is not a guarantee of safety and should be adapted to the situation.

Practical step: List warning signs, trusted contacts, important documents, money/medicines, safe places and emergency options; consider digital safety and children's needs where relevant.

When to seek help: Seek specialist domestic-violence, safeguarding or emergency support when risk is escalating or leaving may increase danger.

◇49Documentation & reportingRights, Safety & Legal Awareness

What to understand: Good documentation can help clarify events and support a complaint, but collecting evidence should not create additional danger.

Practical step: Preserve relevant dates, messages, URLs, names and documents securely; use official reporting channels and keep copies of submissions or reference numbers.

When to seek help: Seek legal or specialist advice before sharing sensitive evidence when doing so could expose you to retaliation, privacy loss or further harm.

✦50Finding verified supportRights, Safety & Legal Awareness

What to understand: Support services vary by country and may include emergency services, health providers, shelters, legal aid, labour authorities, child-protection services and specialist organizations.

Practical step: Start with current government or recognized institutional sources, verify contact details and check whether the service is appropriate for your situation.

When to seek help: If there is immediate danger, use the relevant emergency service; for non-emergency issues, seek qualified local support rather than relying on anonymous viral advice.

Education, Identity & Social Life

♡51Girls’ educationEducation, Identity & Social Life

What to understand: Education can expand knowledge, skills, agency and future options, but access and completion are affected by safety, costs, social norms, care responsibilities, disability and local conditions.

Practical step: Identify the main barrier—fees, transport, safety, household duties, learning support or digital access—and seek a school, community or scholarship pathway that addresses it.

When to seek help: Seek education, safeguarding or legal support when a girl is being prevented from schooling through coercion, violence, exploitation or unlawful discrimination.

◉52Learning confidenceEducation, Identity & Social Life

What to understand: Learning confidence is not fixed; it can grow through practice, feedback, supportive environments and achievable challenges.

Practical step: Break one learning goal into a small weekly task, track progress and ask for specific feedback rather than judging ability from one result.

When to seek help: Seek educational or mental-health support when fear of failure, bullying, severe anxiety or persistent distress blocks participation.

＋53Friendship boundariesEducation, Identity & Social Life

What to understand: Healthy friendships can include trust, respect, reciprocity and room for privacy. Boundaries help define what information, time and behaviour feel acceptable.

Practical step: State one boundary clearly, avoid sharing information you are not ready to share, and notice whether the friendship respects a reasonable limit.

When to seek help: Seek support when a friend uses threats, humiliation, coercion, stalking, exploitation or repeated unwanted contact.

⌁54Social pressureEducation, Identity & Social Life

What to understand: Social pressure can influence choices about appearance, relationships, study, work, marriage, substance use or online behaviour. Its effect varies across people and settings.

Practical step: Pause before agreeing, identify your own values and consequences, and use a trusted person as a reality check when pressure is high.

When to seek help: Seek support when pressure involves threats, coercion, violence, forced marriage, exploitation or serious risk-taking.

◆55Cultural expectationsEducation, Identity & Social Life

What to understand: Cultural traditions can provide belonging and meaning, while expectations can also differ across families, communities and generations. Not every tradition is experienced the same way.

Practical step: Separate what you value from what you feel forced to do; discuss expectations respectfully and identify where personal choice and safety matter.

When to seek help: Seek confidential support when expectations involve coercion, discrimination, violence, forced marriage or denial of essential education, work or healthcare.

⌂56Identity and self-understandingEducation, Identity & Social Life

What to understand: Identity can include values, roles, relationships, culture, interests, goals and personal experiences. It can develop over time rather than being solved by one label.

Practical step: Write down what matters to you, what roles you choose and what pressures you notice; allow room for uncertainty and change.

When to seek help: Seek professional support when identity-related distress is persistent, severe or connected with abuse, self-harm or major impairment.

✿57Leadership confidenceEducation, Identity & Social Life

What to understand: Leadership can involve listening, decision-making, accountability, collaboration and speaking up—not only formal titles. Gender stereotypes can shape opportunities and expectations.

Practical step: Practice one leadership behaviour in a safe setting: facilitate a discussion, organize a task, mentor someone or make a clear evidence-based decision.

When to seek help: Seek mentoring, training or organizational support when discrimination, harassment or exclusion limits participation or advancement.

🛡58Public speaking confidenceEducation, Identity & Social Life

What to understand: Speaking confidence develops with preparation, repeated practice and supportive feedback; nervousness before speaking is common.

Practical step: Prepare three key points, rehearse aloud, slow your pace and use notes or slides strategically; start with low-stakes opportunities.

When to seek help: Seek professional support if severe anxiety, panic or avoidance consistently prevents education, work or important participation.

◇59Healthy friendshipsEducation, Identity & Social Life

What to understand: Healthy friendships generally allow mutual respect, trust, boundaries and room for disagreement without fear. No friendship is conflict-free.

Practical step: Notice whether support and effort are reasonably reciprocal, repair misunderstandings and maintain more than one source of social support.

When to seek help: Seek help when a relationship repeatedly involves manipulation, threats, abuse, exploitation or isolation from other support.

✦60Life transitionsEducation, Identity & Social Life

What to understand: Transitions such as leaving school, marriage, childbirth, relocation, divorce, career change or caregiving can affect routines, identity, finances and relationships.

Practical step: Map what is changing, what remains stable and what practical support you need; set one short-term goal while allowing adjustment time.

When to seek help: Seek professional or community support when transition-related distress becomes severe, persistent, unsafe or seriously disrupts daily functioning.

Research, Myths & Practical Tools

♡61Myth vs factResearch, Myths & Practical Tools

What to understand: A viral claim is not automatically evidence. Health, psychology, relationships and safety claims should be checked against high-quality research, official guidance and the limits of the evidence.

Practical step: Write the claim exactly, identify who is making it, check the date and look for primary research, systematic reviews or authoritative guidance.

When to seek help: Seek a qualified professional for high-stakes health, legal or safety decisions rather than relying on a social-media post.

◉62How to read health claimsResearch, Myths & Practical Tools

What to understand: Health claims can omit uncertainty, absolute risks, study limitations, conflicts of interest and whether a finding applies to you.

Practical step: Ask: What outcome was measured? Compared with what? How large was the effect? Who was studied? Was it randomized or observational? Is the source current?

When to seek help: For symptoms, medication, pregnancy, nutrition or treatment decisions, use current clinical guidance and qualified healthcare advice.

＋63How to check online adviceResearch, Myths & Practical Tools

What to understand: Online advice can mix personal experience, marketing, outdated information and research. A confident tone does not establish accuracy.

Practical step: Check the author, source, date, references and whether independent authoritative sources agree; distinguish an anecdote from evidence.

When to seek help: If advice could affect health, safety, money, legal rights or a child's welfare, verify it before acting.

⌁64Research basicsResearch, Myths & Practical Tools

What to understand: Research quality depends on design, sampling, measurement, analysis, transparency and how well results generalize. One study rarely settles a complex question.

Practical step: Look for systematic reviews or multiple credible studies, inspect the population and outcomes, and note limitations before drawing a conclusion.

When to seek help: Ask a researcher, librarian, clinician or other qualified expert when evidence is conflicting or the decision is high stakes.

◆65Question checklistResearch, Myths & Practical Tools

What to understand: Good questions make it easier to clarify goals, risks, evidence and practical constraints before a decision.

Practical step: Ask: What exactly is happening? What do I know? What is uncertain? What options exist? What are the risks and benefits? Who can verify this?

When to seek help: Use professional advice when the consequences are serious or you cannot safely assess the information yourself.

⌂66Decision reflectionResearch, Myths & Practical Tools

What to understand: Reflection can improve clarity by separating facts, values, assumptions and emotions. It does not guarantee a correct decision.

Practical step: Write the decision, facts, uncertainties, values, options, likely consequences and one reversible next step; revisit after a cooling-off period when appropriate.

When to seek help: Seek trusted or professional support for decisions involving coercion, violence, serious health risks, major finances or legal consequences.

✿67Safety checklistResearch, Myths & Practical Tools

What to understand: Safety planning works best when it is specific to the situation and updated as risks change. A generic checklist cannot cover every emergency.

Practical step: Check immediate danger, trusted contacts, safe locations, transport, documents, medicines, money, device/account security and child/dependent needs where relevant.

When to seek help: Use emergency or specialist services for immediate danger, escalating violence, credible threats or serious safeguarding concerns.

🛡68Relationship reflectionResearch, Myths & Practical Tools

What to understand: Relationship quality is better understood through patterns over time than one isolated disagreement. Respect, autonomy, accountability and repair are useful areas to examine.

Practical step: Record observable patterns: how conflict starts, how each person responds, whether boundaries are respected and whether repair and change follow harm.

When to seek help: Seek specialist support when fear, coercion, violence, stalking or persistent emotional harm is present.

◇69Digital safety checklistResearch, Myths & Practical Tools

What to understand: Digital safety is ongoing because accounts, devices, platforms and threats change. Privacy and security settings should be reviewed periodically.

Practical step: Use unique passwords, MFA/passkeys where available, updated devices, privacy controls, secure recovery methods and evidence preservation when needed.

When to seek help: Seek platform, cybercrime, legal or specialist support for account takeover, doxxing, stalking, threats, blackmail or serious image-based abuse.

✦70Personal action planResearch, Myths & Practical Tools

What to understand: An action plan turns information into a manageable next step while leaving room to revise when new evidence or circumstances appear.

Practical step: Write the goal, one safe next action, deadline, support person/source, possible barrier and review date; keep high-stakes decisions open to qualified advice.
