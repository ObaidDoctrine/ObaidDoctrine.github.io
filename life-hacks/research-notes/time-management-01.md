## Verified evidence used
1. Aeon, Faber & Panaccio (2021), PLOS ONE, "Does time management work? A meta-analysis". DOI: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0245066
   - Full text checked at https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7799745/
   - Abstract reports a meta-analysis of 158 studies and 53,957 participants, with moderate relationships between time management and job performance, academic achievement, and well-being. Wording in article avoids causal overstatement.
   - The paper defines time management in terms of structuring, protecting and adapting time.
2. Patzak, Zhang & Vytasek (2025), Frontiers in Education, "Boosting productivity and wellbeing through time management: evidence-based strategies for higher education and workforce development". DOI: https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2025.1623228
   - Full text checked at https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2025.1623228/full
   - Systematic review synthesizes 107 empirical studies; abstract identifies planning, goal-setting, prioritization and task organization as beneficial strategies. Scope includes higher education and workplace development.
3. Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2024), "The when and how of planning: Meta-analysis of the scope and components of implementation intentions in 642 tests". DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/10463283.2024.2334563
   - Abstract and bibliographic details checked in search result. Publisher page returned 403 when opened, so full text was not independently inspected. Claim limited to the abstract: 642 tests; implementation intentions were effective for cognitive, affective and behavioural outcomes, with effects varying by conditions.
## Editorial cautions
- 15 minutes is an editorially designed practical routine, not a validated clinical protocol or a scientifically established optimum.
- The 3-minute intervals are instructional scaffolding, not evidence-based thresholds.
- Do not promise guaranteed productivity, reduced stress or success.
- Keep examples inclusive of care duties, shift work, unpredictable schedules and limited schedule autonomy.
